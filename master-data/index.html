How I would approach master data product

Product strategy for vendor and product master data at enterprise scale — the core motion for a technology distributor. Golden records, stewardship workflows, governance, and downstream sync. Built for business users and ops teams first, not a portal nobody opens.

Vendor → Product → Customer

I am a product leader with 15+ years in marketplace and transaction systems. Most of that at Auction.com. This page is how I would run a vendor and product MDM line: where to start, what to ship, and what I would measure so leadership can see business value before we ask for the next phase.

The problem

A global distributor runs on product and vendor records across ERP, commerce, CRM, and internal ops tools. When those systems disagree, business users fix it by hand. Trust drops. Quotes, fulfillment, and reporting slow down.

Master data work is not a one-time cleanup. It is ongoing stewardship: duplicates, stale attributes, ownership gaps, and vendor feeds that were never designed for your model.

Where I would start

Vendor and product data first. That is the core motion for a technology distributor. I would pick one business unit, define a golden record for a narrow slice of the catalog, and ship a stewardship queue people will actually open: duplicate detection, clear ownership, approve or fix, audit trail.

External vendors will not standardize because you asked them to. The product job is ingestion rules, normalization, exception handling, and clear accountability. I have seen that with licensee and vendor feeds at Disney and Auction.com. I would expect the same pattern here.

What I would bring from prior work

At Auction.com, nationwide expansion depended on the core internal platform: catalog rules, transaction configuration, and ops workflows that had to hold up across states and teams. Different industry, same failure mode: ops breaks when records are not trusted.

At Disney, partner data rarely arrived in one clean format. We normalized licensee feeds into a warehouse so analytics could be trusted. That is the realistic ingestion mindset MDM needs.

Salesforce sat upstream and downstream of Auction.com's core platform. Useful MDM context: bad vendor and product data upstream still becomes rework downstream, whether the consumer is CRM, ERP, or an internal tool.

Phased approach

Sequencing by dependency and proof, not a calendar. Each phase earns the next when exit criteria and KPIs move.

Phase 1 — Foundation

Golden record and stewardship

Goal: Trustworthy vendor/product records in one business unit, with a workflow ops will use.

Ship

  • Golden record model for vendor + product (narrow catalog slice)
  • Stewardship queue: duplicates, ownership, approve/fix
  • Baseline data quality scorecard by domain
  • Ingestion and normalization rules for top vendor feeds

Exit criteria

  • Measurable quality lift on P0 attributes
  • Reduction in manual correction hours per week
  • Active stewards using the queue weekly

KPIs I'd report

  • P0 completeness & duplication rate (baseline → monthly)
  • Manual correction hours / week (ops-reported)
  • Stewardship task completion rate
Phase 2 — Governance

Definitions, ownership, downstream sync

Goal: Agreed definitions and controlled change, with key systems consuming the same truth.

Ship

  • Data definitions and ownership model (RACI that ops understands)
  • Change workflow for attribute updates and retirements
  • Sync to priority downstream systems (ERP, commerce, CRM)
  • Conflict surfacing when systems disagree

Exit criteria

  • Cross-system match rate on golden record IDs
  • Fewer which-number-is-right escalations
  • Governance council with real decisions, not theater

KPIs I'd report

  • Golden ID match rate across ERP / commerce / CRM
  • Downstream conflict escalations (trend)
  • Time to approve governed attribute changes
Phase 3 — Scale

Lifecycle automation and self-service

Goal: Onboard, change, and retire records with guardrails. Less heroics, more routine.

Ship

  • Automated onboarding for approved vendor/product patterns
  • Self-service requests for business users with policy checks
  • Lifecycle states with downstream propagation
  • Health dashboard for domain owners

Exit criteria

  • Drop in emergency data fixes and one-off scripts
  • Adoption of internal MDM tools across additional business units
  • Time savings documented with ops input

KPIs I'd report

  • Emergency fix volume (trend down)
  • Self-service request volume vs ticket backlog
  • BU adoption of stewardship workflows

Outcomes, KPIs, and business value

I would not ask leadership to fund MDM on faith. Every phase ties program metrics to outcomes the business can feel.

Business outcomes

What leadership should care about

  • Quote-to-cash friction — fewer holds and rework from bad vendor or SKU data
  • Ops cost — manual correction hours trending down
  • Trust — fewer cross-system escalations ("which number is right?")
  • Risk — fulfillment and reporting errors tied to master data (where measurable)

Program metrics

How I would prove progress

  • Data quality by domain: completeness, duplication, stale P0 attributes
  • Stewardship queue completion and time-to-resolve
  • Adoption: corrections through MDM workflow vs email/spreadsheets
  • Downstream golden-record match rate and conflict count
Metric Phase Why it matters
P0 attribute completeness 1 Baseline trust in vendor + product records before sync
Manual correction hours / week 1 Direct ops cost and pain proxy
Golden ID match rate (ERP, commerce, CRM) 2 Proof downstream systems share one truth
Cross-system conflict escalations 2 Trust metric leadership will recognize
Emergency data fixes & one-off scripts 3 Scale without heroics

How I would work cross-functionally

Data engineering on pipeline reliability and idempotent ingest. Enterprise architecture on system-of-record calls that need to be visible, not buried. Business operations as design partners for stewardship UX. Experience design on friction reduction in the tools people already use.

I influence without authority by shipping something small that ops trusts, then expanding scope when pull rate proves it.

How I'd partner with the team

Vendor and product MDM wins when value is visible early and everyone knows what we are optimizing for.

I would baseline quality and rework hours with ops before we ship Phase 1, publish a simple scorecard domain owners review monthly, and tie roadmap calls to whether stewards actually use what we build. If adoption or quality flatlines, we fix the workflow before we add scope.

That keeps engineering, architecture, and business ops aligned on outcomes — not activity. Perfect data is not the goal. Directional lift on P0 vendor and product attributes, with less manual repair and fewer downstream fires, is what earns the next investment.

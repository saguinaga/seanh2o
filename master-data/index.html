How I would approach master data product

Product strategy for vendor and product master data at enterprise scale — the core motion for a technology distributor. Golden records, ops workflows, governance, and downstream sync. Built for business users and ops teams first, not a portal nobody opens.

Vendor → Product → Customer

NYSE: INGM ~1,500 vendors · 161K+ customers Public again since Oct 2024

I am a product leader with 15+ years in marketplace and transaction systems. Most of that at Auction.com. This page is how I would run a vendor and product MDM line: where to start, what to ship, and what I would measure so leadership can see business value before we ask for the next phase.

The problem

Ingram Micro runs on product and vendor records across ERP, commerce (including Xvantage), CRM, and internal ops tools. When those systems disagree, business users fix it by hand. Trust drops. Quotes, fulfillment, and reporting slow down.

Master data work is not a one-time cleanup. It is ongoing ops work: duplicates, stale attributes, ownership gaps, and vendor feeds that were never designed for your model.

Where I would start

Vendor and product data first. That is the core motion for a technology distributor. I would pick one business unit, agree what counts as golden for a narrow slice: which P0 attributes matter, who owns fixes, and what done looks like before touching downstream sync. Then ship an ops queue people will actually open: duplicate detection, clear ownership, approve or fix, audit trail.

External vendors will not standardize because you asked them to. The product job is ingestion rules, normalization, exception handling, and clear accountability. I have seen that with licensee and vendor feeds at Disney and Auction.com. I would expect the same pattern here.

What I would bring from prior work

At Auction.com, nationwide expansion depended on the core internal platform: catalog rules, transaction configuration, and ops workflows that had to hold up across states and teams. Different industry, same failure mode: ops breaks when records are not trusted.

At Disney, partner data rarely arrived in one clean format. We normalized licensee feeds into a warehouse so analytics could be trusted. That is the realistic ingestion mindset MDM needs.

Salesforce sat upstream and downstream of Auction.com's core platform. Useful MDM context: bad vendor and product data upstream still becomes rework downstream, whether the consumer is CRM, ERP, or an internal tool.

Phased approach

Sequencing by dependency and proof, not a calendar. Each phase earns the next when exit criteria and KPIs move. Each item below is a scoped increment, not a platform launch. Phase 1 is one BU, one catalog slice, P0 attributes, and 1–2 vendor feeds.

Phase 1 — Foundation

Golden record and ops queue

Goal: Trustworthy vendor/product records in one business unit, with a workflow ops will use.

First increments

  • Agree golden record definition for vendor + product: P0 attributes only, one BU, one catalog slice
  • MVP ops queue on that slice: surface duplicates, assign owner, approve or fix, audit log
  • Baseline scorecard for the pilot slice (completeness, duplication) before you change anything
  • Normalization rules for 1–2 highest-volume vendor feeds, with a manual exception path for everything else

Exit criteria

  • Directional lift on P0 attributes in the pilot slice
  • Documented drop in manual correction hours on the pilot slice
  • Pilot ops team using the queue weekly without a spreadsheet workaround

KPIs I'd report

  • P0 completeness & duplication rate (baseline → monthly)
  • Manual correction hours / week (ops-reported)
  • Ops queue completion rate
Phase 2 — Governance

Definitions, ownership, downstream sync

Goal: Agreed definitions and controlled change, with key systems consuming the same truth.

First increments

  • Published definitions + RACI for pilot P0 attributes (expand attribute set only when Phase 1 holds)
  • Change workflow for governed P0 updates in the pilot slice
  • Pilot sync to one priority downstream system first, then add the next when match rate holds
  • Conflict report when two systems disagree on golden IDs in the pilot

Exit criteria

  • Golden ID match rate improving in the pilot (baseline → monthly)
  • Downward trend in which-number-is-right escalations on the pilot slice
  • Monthly review with domain owners that results in decisions, not status theater

KPIs I'd report

  • Golden ID match rate across ERP / commerce / CRM
  • Downstream conflict escalations (trend)
  • Time to approve governed attribute changes
Phase 3 — Scale

Lifecycle automation and self-service

Goal: Onboard, change, and retire records with guardrails. Less heroics, more routine.

First increments

  • Templated onboarding for a small set of repeat vendor/product patterns ops already handles by hand
  • Self-service for low-risk attribute changes, with policy checks and an ops escalation path
  • Lifecycle states for pilot entities, with downstream propagation tested on one consumer first
  • Simple health view for domain owners on the pilot slice (not enterprise-wide day one)

Exit criteria

  • Downward trend in emergency fixes on the pilot slice
  • Second BU asks to use the workflow (pull, not mandate)
  • Time savings documented with ops input, not modeled

KPIs I'd report

  • Emergency fix volume (trend down)
  • Self-service request volume vs ticket backlog
  • BU adoption of ops workflows

Outcomes, KPIs, and business value

I would not ask leadership to fund MDM on faith. Every phase ties program metrics to outcomes the business can feel. As a public company again, those metrics need to be legible to leadership — directional lift on P0 attributes and ops hours saved, not activity counts.

Business outcomes

What leadership should care about

  • Quote-to-cash friction — fewer holds and rework from bad vendor or SKU data
  • Ops cost — manual correction hours trending down
  • Trust — fewer cross-system escalations ("which number is right?")
  • Risk — fulfillment and reporting errors tied to master data (where measurable)

Program metrics

How I would prove progress

  • Data quality by domain: completeness, duplication, stale P0 attributes
  • Ops queue completion and time-to-resolve
  • Adoption: corrections through MDM workflow vs email/spreadsheets
  • Downstream golden-record match rate and conflict count
Metric Phase Why it matters
P0 attribute completeness 1 Baseline trust in vendor + product records before sync
Manual correction hours / week 1 Direct ops cost and pain proxy
Golden ID match rate (ERP, commerce, CRM) 2 Proof downstream systems share one truth
Cross-system conflict escalations 2 Trust metric leadership will recognize
Emergency data fixes & one-off scripts 3 Scale without heroics

How I would work cross-functionally

Data engineering on pipeline reliability and idempotent ingest. Enterprise architecture on system-of-record calls that need to be visible, not buried. Business operations as design partners for ops workflow UX. Experience design on friction reduction in the tools people already use.

I influence without authority by shipping something small that ops trusts, then expanding scope when pull rate proves it.

How I'd partner with the team

Vendor and product MDM wins when value is visible early and everyone knows what we are optimizing for.

I would baseline quality and rework hours with ops before we ship Phase 1, publish a simple scorecard domain owners review monthly, and tie roadmap calls to whether ops actually use what we build. If adoption or quality flatlines, we fix the workflow before we add scope.

That keeps engineering, architecture, and business ops aligned on outcomes — not activity. Perfect data is not the goal. Directional lift on P0 vendor and product attributes, with less manual repair and fewer downstream fires, is what earns the next investment.

I would not pretend to run the whole stack. In vendor and product MDM, the job is to prove value per phase before the next investment — baseline with ops, ship what ops use, expand when KPIs move. That is how this lane contributes to catalog trust and quote-to-cash at distributor scale.

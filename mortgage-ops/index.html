How I would approach specialty mortgage ops analytics

Business optimization seat next to the lending business: translate executive needs into Salesforce-backed analytics and a backlog people can ship while the book is live. Public framing uses Redwood Trust platforms (including CoreVest business-purpose lending and Aspire non-QM) as the market context. This is an approach, not a claim that I work there.

Definitions → Pipeline truth → Time to yes → Scale views

NYSE: RWT Specialty housing credit BPL · non-QM · jumbo / distribution

Fifteen years at Auction.com on loan-centric, highly regulated transaction systems. Salesforce as a product surface, not as an admin craft. This page is how I would bring value in an Accelerator-style role: find blind spots, lock metric definitions, and make internal dashboards serve the moments that decide whether a broker or investor chooses your firm next time.

Why this seat matters

Specialty mortgage platforms do not win only on rate. In business-purpose and non-QM markets especially, people choose (and re-choose) a firm when three things hold: product fit for the deal, speed with certainty to a real yes, and a low-friction path for the broker, correspondent, or investor who has to live in your process.

Rate and guidelines get you into the conversation. What loses deals after that is usually operational: opaque status, slow condition clearing, inconsistent decisions, weak feedback when a file stalls, and internal teams arguing about which report is true. Analytics that only count locked volume miss that story.

The product job in a business-optimization / BA+ seat is not to pretend you are Capital Markets. It is to sit with the business, name the decision moments, and make Salesforce data trustworthy enough that leadership can see friction early and engineering can fix the right bottlenecks.

What I know about “why us vs them”

Not proprietary to any one lender. Pattern language from marketplace and transaction work, plus how specialty housing credit publicly positions itself (liquidity, underserved segments, operational scale).

Speed that is real

Time to clear conditions, time to credit decision, time to fund. Fake speed (marketing SLAs with no data) erodes trust faster than a slow honest timeline.

Certainty of close

Investors and brokers care whether “yes” sticks. Last-minute re-trades and surprise overlays push volume to the competitor who is boringly consistent.

Product fit

Bridge, rental / DSCR, build-for-rent, non-QM income stories: the firm that understands the deal structure without forcing it into a consumer QM box wins the relationship.

Seller / broker experience

Submission friction, status visibility, who to call, how exceptions get answered. Correspondents and brokers multi-home. Experience is a retention product.

Definitional trust

If ops, sales, and finance disagree on “in pipeline,” “stuck,” or “time to yes,” dashboards become politics. Shared definitions are the product foundation.

Regulated calm

Auditability, consistent disclosures, clean handoffs. In regulated credit, sloppy process is both a risk and a reputation cost with sophisticated counterparties.

My hypothesis for an Accelerator seat: the competitive surface is external (broker, investor, borrower-adjacent parties), but the leverage is internal. Salesforce analytics should answer where time and certainty break, not only how much we locked last week.

How I would bring value in the first stretch

Sequencing by proof, not a calendar. Each phase earns the next when definitions hold and someone actually uses the view.

Phase 0–1 — Foundation

Decision moments and metric dictionary

Goal: One product line (or one funnel slice), agreed stage language, and a baseline that matches how the business talks.

What I would do

  • Map who needs what decision weekly (exec, sales lead, ops, credit)
  • Write the dictionary: application in, credit decision, cleared to close, funded, dead, recycled
  • Pick one slice (example: business-purpose bridge path) and baseline aging + conversion from real SF data
  • List top 5 “stuck” reasons in human language, not only picklist codes

Exit criteria

  • Two leaders use the same stage definitions in a meeting without a side debate
  • Baseline time-in-stage and conversion published for the pilot slice
  • Backlog of 3–5 instrumentation or process gaps ranked by impact on time to yes

What I would report

  • Median / p90 time to decision and time to fund (pilot slice)
  • Volume of files past SLA by stage
  • % of files with missing critical fields that block stage advance
Phase 2 — Operating views

Dashboards people open on Monday

Goal: Exception-first views for the pilot, not a wall of charts.

What I would do

  • Aging and exception queues tied to owners, not anonymous pipeline charts
  • Funnel with drop-off reasons; partner with ops on root causes
  • Source-of-truth rules when SF and adjacent systems disagree
  • Light user interviews: where people still keep a spreadsheet

Exit criteria

  • Ops or sales lead stops maintaining a parallel tracker for the pilot slice
  • Weekly review runs off the SF-backed view
  • At least one process or field change shipped from the data

What I would report

  • Exception volume trend and time-to-clear
  • Conversion by stage vs baseline
  • Definition drift incidents (zero is the goal)
Phase 3 — Expand

Second product line and cleaner handoffs

Goal: Reuse the dictionary pattern; only then widen dashboards.

What I would do

  • Clone the method for a second path (example: term rental / DSCR or non-QM correspondent intake)
  • Handoff and status visibility requirements that serve external partners
  • Integration edge cases when numbers break trust (request/response, failed writes)

Exit criteria

  • Shared components for stages and exception types where products truly share meaning
  • No silent redefinition of “time to yes” across lines

What I would report

  • Cross-product comparison only where definitions match
  • Partner-facing status accuracy (where measurable)

Sample questions I would ask in the first weeks

  • Which decision is late most often: credit, conditions, or funding ops?
  • What does “time to yes” mean here in writing, and who owns the definition?
  • Where do people still leave Salesforce to track work?
  • Which partner complaints repeat, and can we see them in stage data?
  • When two systems disagree on a loan file, which one is allowed to win?

What I would not do on day one

  • Build an enterprise dashboard wall before stage definitions hold for one slice
  • Claim full mortgage lifecycle mastery I do not have; learn their products on purpose
  • Confuse admin config volume with product outcomes
  • Optimize vanity lock volume if certainty and cycle time are the real competitive story

Bottom line

I am not selling myself as a career capital-markets PM. I am a systems and product person who has lived in loan-centric, regulated, multi-party operations with Salesforce in the middle. The value I would bring is turning “we need better dashboards” into shared language, trustworthy pipeline truth, and a backlog that shortens real time to yes for the people who choose a specialty lender over another.

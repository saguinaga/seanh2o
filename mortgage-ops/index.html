    Why this seat is Salesforce-shaped

    Specialty mortgage platforms do not win only on rate. Brokers, correspondents, and investors re-choose a firm when product fit, real speed to yes, and a low-friction path hold. In a regulated shop those moments also have to be explainable: who changed what, which disclosure or KYC hold is open, whether the CRM and the adjacent system still agree.

    The BA+ / Accelerator job is not Capital Markets theater. It is sitting with the business, locking what “Application in,” “decisioned,” and “stuck” mean on the record, then using Salesforce reports, dashboards, and custom components so leadership sees friction early. When canned reports are wrong, dig (SOQL, field history, integration logs), fix definitions, then rebuild the component. Prefer human judgment on holds; use AI to prioritize the queue, not to invent the decision.

    Proof I already did regulated at scale: contracts and offers

    At Auction.com we were not in a casual paperwork lane. The business ran on loans (sellers were lenders and servicers putting inventory through the channel). Different loan types needed different contracts, and those contracts had to generate at the offer level, not as a one-size PDF someone edited by hand under pressure.

    I worked heavily on automated contract generation: property situation, loan type, and jurisdiction (state, county, city) drove which disclosures and language went into the package. Nationwide volume only works if the rules engine and ops workflow stay aligned. That process had to hold across a large multi-lender seller base (on the order of 100+ lenders / institutional sellers on the platform), each with their own expectations, audits, and “don’t break our compliance posture” pressure.

    Related regulated surface: auction cry language that varied by location, generated dynamically, shown on a mobile path, recorded, and retrievable when legal needed the exact words that were read. Same pattern as good CRM exception design: the system must produce the right artifact, leave a trail, and let humans handle the exceptions that automation should not invent.

    That is the bridge into specialty mortgage analytics. I am not claiming I ran CoreVest product guidelines. I am claiming I have already lived multi-party, multi-jurisdiction, loan-type-driven document and process rules under bank and counterparty scrutiny, and I know how easy it is for “just generate the package” to become a compliance incident when definitions and data are wrong.

    Prototype: Exception Queue (custom component)

    Open Exceptions in the chrome (or waffle → Exception Queue). AI scores open items by compliance tags, SLA breach, partner tier, and size band. People claim, escalate to compliance, or resolve with a note that lands in a sample action log. That is the regulated version of “time to yes”: speed where it is safe, escalation where control requires a human. Same muscle as contract/offer automation: rules and data first, humans on the edge cases.

    What chooses one firm over another

    Pattern language for specialty housing credit. Competitive surface is external; leverage for this seat is what Salesforce can prove about the path.

    Speed that is real

    Time in stage and time to decision you can defend in a report. Marketing SLAs with no CRM data erode trust.

    Certainty of close

    Re-decision rate, fallout after approval, condition churn. “Yes” that sticks is a pipeline metric, not a slogan.

    Product fit

    Bridge, rental / DSCR, build-for-rent, specialty credit: product and channel fields must be reportable.

    Seller / broker path

    Submission completeness, kickbacks, status visibility. Experience issues should map to stage reasons in SF.

    Definitional trust

    If sales, ops, and finance disagree on stage meaning, every dashboard becomes politics. Dictionary first.

    Regulated calm

    Disclosures, KYC holds, dual-system status, reason codes. Field history and action logs matter when examiners or counterparties ask “who decided?”

    How I would bring value (Salesforce delivery sequence)

    Each phase earns the next when stage language holds and someone runs the meeting off CRM, not a side spreadsheet.

    Phase 0–1 · Foundation

    Metric dictionary on the object model

    Goal: One product slice. Written stage meanings. Baseline report that matches how leaders talk.

    What I would do

    • Map decision moments to the live loan-file object and key fields
    • Write the dictionary: app in, decisioned, cleared, funded, dead, recycled
    • Baseline conversion and time-in-stage from real SF data (report or SOQL)
    • Name top stuck reasons in human language, not only picklist codes

    Exit criteria

    • Two leaders use the same stage names without a side debate
    • Pilot report published and trusted for one weekly review
    • 3–5 field or process gaps ranked by impact on time to yes

    Salesforce artifacts

    • Stage definitions doc + field glossary
    • Summary report: volume and aging by stage
    • List view draft: past-SLA exceptions
    Phase 2 · Operating layer

    Dashboards and queues people open

    Goal: Exception-first Lightning dashboard for the pilot, fed by trusted reports.

    What I would do

    • Dashboard components: aging, funnel, stuck reasons, owner load
    • List views as working queues (owner + SLA), not vanity charts only
    • SoT rules when SF and LOS / warehouse disagree
    • Find where people still export to Excel and why

    Exit criteria

    • Weekly ops/sales review runs from the SF dashboard
    • Parallel tracker retired for the pilot slice
    • At least one field or process change shipped from the data

    Salesforce artifacts

    • Dashboard (pilot) with 4–6 components
    • Joined or custom report types as needed
    • Backlog stories with AC for platform/admin partners
    Phase 3 · Expand

    Second product path, same grammar

    Goal: Reuse stage grammar; only then clone dashboards. No silent redefinition of time to yes.

    What I would do

    • Second path (e.g. term rental / DSCR or another specialty product)
    • Shared components only where stage meaning truly matches
    • Integration edges that break CRM trust (failed writes, dual status)

    Exit criteria

    • Cross-product compare only on aligned definitions
    • Definition drift incidents stay at zero

    Salesforce artifacts

    • Foldered reports by product line
    • Optional path guidance on key stages
    • Integration exception report

    Questions I would ask in the first weeks

    • Which object is the system of work for a live file: custom Loan, Opportunity-shaped, both?
    • What does “time to yes” mean in field and stage terms, and who owns that definition?
    • Which reports does leadership already distrust, and why?
    • Where do people still leave Salesforce (Excel, email, chat) to track work?
    • When SF and another system disagree, which record is allowed to win?
    • Who builds report types and dashboards day to day: admins, analysts, or vendors?

    What I would not do on day one

    • Ship a 20-component dashboard before stage definitions hold for one slice
    • Claim full mortgage lifecycle mastery; learn product guidelines on purpose
    • Confuse admin config volume with product outcomes
    • Optimize lock volume if certainty and cycle time are the competitive story

    Bottom line

    I am not selling capital-markets PM credentials. I am a systems and product person who has lived in loan-centric, regulated operations with Salesforce in the middle. Value is turning “we need better dashboards” into stage language, trustworthy reports, list-view queues, and a backlog that shortens real time to yes for the people who choose a specialty lender over another.

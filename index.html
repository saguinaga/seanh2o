About Me

I'm Sean Aguinaga. I've spent most of my career on the messy side of real estate tech: marketplaces, bidding, registration, post-sale workflows, and the internal tools that are supposed to make all of that coherent.

I think in systems. Not architecture diagrams for their own sake, but the full loop: what the customer experiences, what data we actually have, what the team in SFDC sees, and where those stories diverge. That's usually where the product work is.

I'm confident in what I know and quick to say when I'm still filling in gaps. I'd rather get the problem framed right than ship something that sounds good in a deck.

My Mission

Real estate is still harder than it needs to be. Not because people aren't trying, but because the journey is long, the data is fragmented, and customers end up re-explaining themselves at every step.

I want products that give customers a clear path forward: sign up once, get treated like we remember them, and see next steps that match their situation. And I want internal teams to have enough context to help without putting the customer on hold while someone digs through five systems.

If we get those two things closer together, the experience gets faster for customers and less chaotic for the people doing the work. That's the mission, basically.

Current Focus

At Auction.com I'm leading product work on two problems I think are upstream of almost everything else: account creation that actually sets up personalization, and better journey context for teams working with customers nationwide.

01

Registration & Personalization

Most customers still hit generic flows before we know much about them. I'm working on registration and account setup so we capture the right profile signals early and the product can respond from there.

That means fewer dead ends in sign-up, preferences that persist, and a real baseline for relevance before someone has to call in and explain who they are again.

02

Journey Context for Teams

Our teams are spread across the country. Customers move through a long journey. When someone picks up the phone or opens a case, the person on the other end should already know where they are, what's still open, and what happened last.

I'm focused on surfacing that in a way that's useful in the moment. Not another dashboard. Context you can act on, whether you're in SFDC, email, or on a call with a customer who's been waiting on an answer.

The product and the human conversation ought to tell the same story. That's the thread I'm pulling on right now.

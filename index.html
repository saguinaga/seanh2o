About

Nearly 20 years in software and product. Deep in the work, not high on the org chart. Real estate and auctions have been the long chapter, but the problems repeat: multi-sided platforms, messy operations, configuration and data that has to hold up across states and teams.

Earlier: Disney (partner data into a warehouse so analytics could be trusted) and Boeing (engineering-adjacent tooling). Good systems work starts with how data and work actually move, not a clean slide.

How I work

The customer isn't just the login.
Brokers, lenders, internal ops, and front-line operators all have to succeed for the product to stick. I design for the full chain, not a single persona slide.
Talk to users before you scale.
Especially for third-party tools: shadow the workflow, validate which fields move decisions, ship where pull rate proves adoption - not where a roadmap deck says you should.
Meet people in their tools.
Excel, Word, Salesforce, cloud folders. Not a new portal nobody asked for. Context should show up where work already happens.

Work

Auction.com experience, then case studies and tools you can open.

Experience · Auction.com · ~15 years

In-person foreclosure auctions grew from a California pilot into a major line of business. My seat was mostly the system underneath that growth, with real time on buyer and seller surfaces when the work required it.

Primary focus

Operational backbone

Catalog rules, transaction configuration, ops workflows, and the unglamorous backend office tools that keep daily work moving. Helped carry the platform through stack modernization and nationwide expansion. Operators and internal teams were the day-to-day customers.

Same system

When the job needed the other seat

User-facing work on the same marketplace: remote bidding, contracting and closing paths, self-serve seller flows, and related consumer surfaces. Not the whole job. Part of shipping a complete transaction system.
